Aggregate loss, computed in your browser

Fit distributions to claims data, or pick them yourself. The page then builds the total loss twice, with Panjer recursion and with FFT, reads off VaR and TVaR, and prices it. The tail beyond VaR is marked in red.

Upload or paste claim sizes and claim counts; a sample is loaded to start with. The page fits twelve size and four count distributions by maximum likelihood, tests and ranks them, and the selected fits, the best by AIC unless you pick another, drive the model below. Your data never leaves your browser.

How the fitting works

Claim sizes: exponential, gamma, lognormal, Weibull, normal, inverse Gaussian, Pareto (Lomax), log-logistic, Burr XII, GEV, scaled beta (upper bound estimated as the largest value × (n + 1)/n) and a two-component lognormal mixture fitted by EM. Closed-form or Newton estimates are used where they exist, and Nelder–Mead on the log-likelihood otherwise.

Claim counts: Poisson, binomial (n by profile likelihood), negative binomial (r by profile likelihood) and geometric, tested with a chi-square test after merging cells with fewer than five expected periods.

Kolmogorov–Smirnov p-values use the asymptotic distribution, which is conservative when the parameters come from the same data. That is why the Anderson–Darling p-value can be bootstrapped: 199 samples are drawn from the fitted distribution, refitted and tested, as in my thesis.

Check: on my PKM 2025 survey data, this page reproduces the article: a binomial with n = 2 and p = 0.3656 for damage counts, and a lognormal with mean Rp169,064.29 for repair costs.

How the page computes this

Claim counts: binomial (q = 0.25, n = 4·E[N]), Poisson, negative binomial (r = 3) and geometric (r = 1). All four belong to the (a, b, 0) class, so Panjer recursion applies: fS(s) = [1 − a fX(0)]−1 Σj=1…s (a + bj/s) fX(j) fS(s − j).

Claim sizes, all with mean 1: gamma; Pareto (Lomax) with tail index above 2; Weibull; lognormal; GEV of the Fréchet type with shape below 0.5 and lower bound 0; beta with shapes (a, 2) scaled to [0, (a + 2)/a]; and a two-component lognormal mixture with component means in a 1 : 4 ratio.

Grid of 4,096 points (8,192 for heavier tails), rounding-method discretization; FFT with exponential tilting and four-fold padding. VaRp is the smallest grid loss with FS(s) ≥ p, and TVaRp = VaRp + E[(S − VaRp)+] / (1 − p).

Premium: twelve independent months of claims stay in the same (a, b, 0) family (Poisson with 12λ, binomial with 12n trials, negative binomial with 12r), so the annual distribution is built by FFT on a fine grid of up to 65,536 points. Pure premium = expected annual claims × (1 + inflation), discounted half a year at the investment return. Risk loading: cost of capital × (VaR or TVaR − expected claims), α × standard deviation, or θ × expected claims. Gross premium = (pure premium + risk loading) ÷ (1 − operating costs), shown per participant per month and as a share of the wage.

Managing the fund

  1. Liquidity first. Claims must be paid within days, so keep at least the liquid amount above in instruments you can withdraw quickly: sharia savings, short sharia time deposits or a sharia money market mutual fund.
  2. The investment share goes to low-risk sharia fixed income, such as government retail sukuk (the SR and ST series) or a sharia fixed-income mutual fund, ideally with something maturing every year.
  3. No individual stocks for the tabarru' fund. Prices can fall in the same year claims spike, and a forced sale would lock in the loss. Members who want equity exposure can invest their returned surplus personally.
  4. Check every product is registered with and supervised by OJK, and buy through a bank or a registered selling agent (APERD).
  5. Govern it openly: separate accounts for tabarru', ujrah and investment, two signatories from the KUB, and a monthly report to members.

Educational illustration, not investment advice. Returns are assumptions you can change above.

How the calculator works

Damage rate per boat = events ÷ boats observed × risk factors; the kelurahan factor also scales the average repair cost by what the PKM survey observed there. Repair costs follow a lognormal distribution with the average and spread you enter, capped at the boat value; a total loss pays the boat value. Pure premium = expected repair cost per boat a year. The tabarru' share is the pure premium plus the safety margin; contribution = tabarru' share ÷ (1 − ujrah − investment share). With PKM's numbers (E[N] = 0.7312, E[X] = Rp169,064.29, 75 / 10 / 15 split) this gives Rp164,822.53 a year, or Rp13,735.21 a month.

The group's annual claims are computed exactly with FFT on a fine grid: binomial counts with at most two events per boat (or Poisson), lognormal repair costs and optional total losses. Fake claims add extra claims that verification fails to catch. The minimum group size is the smallest number of members for which the chance of a short year stays within your target. The 5-year projection samples 2,000 paths from that distribution and follows PKM's surplus rule: 40% back to members, 25% to the KUB as manager and 35% to collective savings, which absorbs short years together with the investment pot.

How the lab computes this

Daily log returns from closing prices, on the dates every loaded series shares. Historical VaR and TVaR read the empirical loss distribution (overlapping sums for 10 days); Normal uses the mean and standard deviation; Student-t fits location, scale and degrees of freedom by maximum likelihood and simulates 10,000 outcomes. A loss is 1 − eR of the value, so it never exceeds 100%.

Portfolios are rebalanced daily. The minimum-variance and maximum-Sharpe portfolios are long-only and found exactly by checking every subset of stocks. CAPM beta is the slope of each stock's daily return on the index's; the CAPM return is rf + β × (index return − rf). Kupiec's proportion-of-failures test uses a chi-square with one degree of freedom.

Check: on my project's TBLA, LSIP and SMAR prices, the minimum-variance portfolio puts 53.1% in SMAR, against 53.5% in the original analysis.

How the model works

Expected claim cost E(c) = p(sick) × daily benefit × days + p(death) × death benefit + p(disability) × disability benefit + p(sick) × handling cost; the COD rider adds p(failure) × deliveries × value. Gross premium = (1 + administration + risk margin) × E(c) ÷ (1 − commission − other expenses − profit).

Technical provisions at launch, the Bornhuetter–Ferguson starting point before any claims have developed: unearned premium (UPR) = premium × policies; expected ultimate loss (EUL) = E(c) × policies; IBNR = a share of EUL; outstanding claims (OCR) = EUL + IBNR; catastrophe buffer = a share of OCR; total (TTP) = UPR + OCR + buffer.

Projection: policies × (1 − lapse) × (1 + growth) a year; net cash flow = premium − operating cost − claims; capital = target RBC × TTP, with TTP following the policies in force. Present values use the discount rate, and the break-even year is when cumulative net cash flow first covers the year-1 capital.
